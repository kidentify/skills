The rule

Loot boxes are now a prohibited content category for minors.

Under Article 20, games with paid loot boxes must verify a user's age — or restrict the feature by default until verification happens. Article 2 defines a loot box narrowly: a paid, randomized purchase whose contents aren't known in advance. That definition splits the market into tiers.

Exempt
Tier 0 + Tier 1
Free randomized rewards

Daily login crates, gacha pulls earned in-game, randomized quest drops. No payment, no obligation. Odds disclosure still applies.

Verify or Block
Tier 2
Paid, closed-loop, cosmetic only

Skins-only crates, cosmetic gacha. Bought with real money but no gameplay advantage. Age verification required to engage.

Highest Risk
Tier 3
Paid + gameplay impact

Paid randomized power, pay-to-win. Age verification required. ANPD has flagged paid loot boxes as a top-priority enforcement target.

For all tiers: odds and item descriptions must be made clear and accessible — independent of whether age verification applies.

What studios are doing

Three distinct strategies have emerged — and ANPD has signalled where it will push.

Drawn from Neimo's Legal Horizons February + March 2026 briefings on ANPD engagement. The market is bifurcating the way Belgium did with loot boxes a few years ago — except Brazil is a top-5 gaming market, so "just leave" carries a different cost.

Strategy 01

Pull the feature, or pull the title

A meaningful cohort of studios with loot-box-driven monetization are either disabling loot boxes for Brazilian users or pulling the title from the market entirely. Same playbook as the post-2018 Belgium exit. Simple, low-engineering, blunt.

Cost · Lose the LATAM #1 market

Strategy 02

Self-declare and wait

The default for most studios today. Keep the existing "I confirm I am over 18" checkbox. ANPD has explicitly flagged this as the cohort it will target first. Fines aren't immediate, but compliance letters are flying now and private class actions are already a live risk.

Risk · Initial enforcement target
Used by · The silent majority, ~70%
verified_user
Strategy 03

Install real age assurance

Gate the loot-box feature specifically behind age verification — not the whole game. ANPD endorses on-device processing, verifiable credentials, and zero-knowledge proofs. Studios going this route keep the market and convert the regulation into a parental-trust signal.

Upside · Keep the market, keep monetization
Used by · Larger studios + platforms
What ANPD wants — and doesn't

The regulator has been unusually specific about how to verify.

ANPD's preliminary guidance and the March 2026 Presidential Decree together draw a sharp line: privacy-preserving age assurance is welcomed; heavy-handed biometric collection and ID-doc dumps are not.

Enforcement window

Fines aren't immediate. Letters and lawsuits already are.

ANPD won't issue ECA Digital-specific fines until its administrative regulations finalize. But "no fines yet" does not mean "no risk now."

Feb 12, 2026
ANPD industry engagement meeting

Regulator signals it will target self-declaration-only games first. Endorses on-device and interoperable age verification.

Mar 17, 2026
ECA Digital enters into force

Article 20 active. ANPD begins sending compliance letters. Private plaintiffs and other regulators can litigate now — they don't have to wait for ANPD's rules.

Aug 2026
Expected ANPD guidance on age assurance

Specific technical and procedural standards for how verification must be implemented. The window to influence those standards is open now.

Jan 2027
ANPD begins issuing ECA Digital-specific fines

Up to R$50,000,000 per offence or 10% of Brazilian turnover. Brazil has a documented history of aggressive private class-action enforcement on top of regulator action.

The studios investing in verification now are buying the market the others are leaving.

Three strategies. One of them keeps a top-5 gaming market open while the others either lose it or wait for the first fine. The Q1 2026 numbers say the field is wide.
